Behind Closed Doors: Presidential Meetings and Strategic Public Disclosure

Sole-authored job market paper

Abstract

The public cannot fully observe what the U.S. president is working on, and the president chooses what to communicate publicly. I examine what drives this strategic disclosure and its capital-market implications. Using Presidential Daily Diaries, I reconstruct the president's minute-by-minute schedule from 1935-2009 and use an LLM-assisted process to identify potential policy topics discussed in meetings and public communications. I find that public communications more closely reflect meeting topics involving voter priorities and less closely reflect politically divisive topics. Greater correspondence is associated with lower subsequent aggregate and firm-level market uncertainty, with patterns varying by firms' policy exposure and private information access.
